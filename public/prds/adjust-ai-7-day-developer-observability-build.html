PRD: Adjust AI -- 7-Day Developer Observability Build

§ 1 Product Thesis

You said you want to "build up your own knowledge so I can be more involved with my development team." The fix is standing observation, not standing meetings.

By Day 7, every weekday morning, you open a single brief in the channel you already check, and you know exactly what your team is doing: who shipped what, where the time went, what merged, what's stuck. No PR-reading, no nagging, no daily standups. The brief speaks plainly -- you don't need to grow a parallel engineering brain, you need a translator.

This is the canonical GBAutomation 7-day, 3-step Hermes multi-agent build, adapted to Adjust AI's repos.

§ 2 Why Now (in your words)

"I want to build up my own knowledge so I can be more involved with my development team."

You don't want to micromanage. You want to see. That's a different problem than "track velocity" or "do code reviews" -- it's an information problem, not a process problem. The fix is asymmetric: one-time build, daily payoff forever.

§ 3 What You Get on Day 7

Three concrete artifacts. All running unattended after Day 7.

Artifact 1 -- Daily Morning Brief

Lands in your channel (email / Telegram / Slack / Discord -- your call) by your wake-up. Format roughly:

Adjust AI -- 2026-06-08 (Mon)

========================================


Shipped overnight: 4 commits, 1 PR merged



Who: Alice (3), Bob (1)


What: auth-refactor branch -> main; new /healthz endpoint


Top file churn: src/auth/jwt.ts (+87 -42)



Anomaly flags:


  - Bob's 1AM commit to main is unusual (he merges in the morning normally)



Yesterday's open PRs (2): #142 (Alice, 3d open), #145 (Carol, fresh)

Artifact 2 -- Per-Developer Weekly Digest

Every Monday morning, a one-page roll-up per developer for the prior week. Commits, PRs, file-areas touched, rough estimate of "where they spent time." Read it in 90 seconds for a team of 5.

Artifact 3 -- Queryable History

A small searchable archive (Supabase under the hood) so you can ask: "how often did we deploy last month?" or "what was Carol working on the week of May 12?" Returned as plain English, not a SQL dashboard.

§ 4 The 3-Step Build (the 7 days)

Step 1 -- Connect + Scope (Days 1-2)

  • Webhook into your GitHub org (or scheduled polls if you'd rather avoid webhooks)
  • 30-min call: which repos, which devs are in scope, what do you call your branches, what's the dev's normal cadence
  • Spec the agent persona -- voice, length, what counts as an "anomaly"

Step 2 -- Build the Multi-Agent Team (Days 3-5)

Three Hermes agents in concert (the proven pattern):

  • commit-watcher -- listens to GitHub events, normalizes commits + PRs into a canonical row in Supabase
  • brief-composer -- a Claude/Hermes agent that reads the day's events, writes the brief, applies your voice spec
  • delivery agent -- fan-out to email + Telegram + Slack as you've configured

Each agent is independently testable. The team itself is configured via a YAML spec we'll commit to your repo so it's transparent.

Step 3 -- Dry-Run + Tune (Days 6-7)

  • Wire the pipeline to fire briefs to only you for two days
  • You read them, mark "too much" / "too little" / "wrong angle"
  • I tune the brief-composer prompt, retest, ship

By end of Day 7, the daily brief is firing on its real schedule, in your real channel, with your team's real activity. We do a 30-min handoff so you know how to tweak.

§ 6 What I Need From You

Five quick answers -- can be inline on tomorrow's call, no homework required:

  1. Repo URLs / GitHub org -- which org name, public or private, roughly how many active repos?
  2. Dev team -- size + names + roles, so the brief can attribute correctly
  3. Where the brief lands -- email / Slack / Telegram / Discord / multiple?
  4. Brief cadence -- daily, every-other-day, 2x/week? Tradeoffs both ways.
  5. Granularity -- per-developer named metrics, or team-aggregate only? This is a culture question more than a technical one -- some teams want it visible, some want it summarized.

§ 8 Risks + Mitigations

RiskMitigation
GitHub rate limits if a repo is hugeWebhook-first design (push events to us, we don't poll every minute)
Raw lines-of-code is noisy and a bad metric on its ownBrief weights by file-area touched + PR review depth, not just LOC
"My team will feel surveilled" politicsDefault is Max-only delivery; opt-in to share with team. Per-dev names omitted if you choose "aggregate only" in § 6 Q5
Team uses GitLab/Bitbucket, not GitHubOut of scope for v1 -- v2 can add. Flag tomorrow if this is the actual situation
Brief becomes wallpaper / Max stops reading itDay-7 dry-run + voice tuning specifically prevents this. We iterate on what shows up.

§ 9 Out of Scope (for the first 7 days)

  • Code review automation / suggested PR comments
  • On-call paging / incident alerting
  • Performance benchmarking (build times, test latency, deploy frequency aggregates)
  • GitLab / Bitbucket adapters
  • Mobile app / dedicated UI -- email + Telegram are the v1 surfaces
  • Editing or commenting back into your repos

All of the above are Sprint-2-able if you want them after Day 7.

§ 10 Success Criteria

Day 14 (one week post-handoff), all three of these are true:

  • [ ] The daily brief landed in your channel every weekday at the configured time, no manual restarts
  • [ ] You answered "what shipped this week" to someone (yourself, your board, a customer) without opening GitHub
  • [ ] At least one real anomaly catch during the dry-run or Week-1 (e.g. a 1AM merge that turned out to need attention)
